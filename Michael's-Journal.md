# September 20: 

**total time spent 4 hours** <br />



First, I was working on the design for the DJ controller using figma. I started with choosing the initial positions of all
the different controls in order. I divided the Controller into 3 sections: 2 decks on both the right and left sides of the
controller, then there was the mixer placed as the middle section. I designed the whole controller to be 600mm wide and 350mm
long, allowing us to make each of the decks to be 240mm wide and the mixer to be 120mm wide. Inside deck 1 (the left side deck)
I added the Performance pads including 8 38*30mm Push button momentary switch and 4 18*12 push button momentary switches. 
Then I added one rotary potentiometer forming the smart CFX, a slide potentiometer to form the Tempo, and a Loop consisting of
5 diifferent pads. Finally I added the rotary encoder. I repeated almost the same process for deck 2 (the right side deck).

After that I started working on the mixer (the middle section between deck 1 and deck 2). I added the Beat FX consisting of 2 
pads and 2 rotary potentiometers. Then I added the Mic Input including 2 rotary potentiometers and one pad. Then, I added the 
Master consisting of 1 rotary potentiometer. Then I added the Level and headphones controls including 11 rotary potentiometers
and 2 pads. I then added 1 sliding potentiometer for the smart fader and 1 other sliding potentiometer for the crossfader and 2
extra potentiometer for some extra functions.

After finishing the initial design, I worked on improving the design. I divided the DJ controller into sub sections, allowing 
for easier navigation. Second, I started adding the names of each subsection, ensuring the improved navigation, then I added
the names of most pads and potentiometers, providing further improved navigation for the rest of my team and especially my friend
responsible for turning this design into a 3D model. 

For better visual representation, I added colored strokes to most pads and potentiometers representing the RGB lighting effects
of each function, emitating what will be implemented in the actual irl device, this was represented with colors including red, blue,
green, yellow, orange, purple, and gray. I then started adding a corner radius change to pads, this used different corner radius
changes, some valued at a 5% change and some valued at a 3% change. This happened with the goal of providing the closest visual
representation of the irl device. 

Now, for further functional navigation, I started adding small white circles for rotary potentiometers showcasing the actual 
size and placement position of the actual actuator inside of the 3D printed visual component. Same thing happened for the sliding
potentiometers, as I added small squares representing the actual actuator again.
